Technical and quality requirements

Specify the video conditions that make a collection usable.

A strong video-data brief explains what happens on screen, when it happens, how the footage is captured, which variations matter, how labels are defined, and what evidence accompanies delivery.

Capture plan

  • Actions, events, routines, settings, or interaction categories
  • Participants, objects, locations, and inclusion or exclusion rules
  • Camera devices, framing, viewpoints, lighting, resolution, and duration
  • Target variation, edge cases, and sampling considerations

Temporal annotation and metadata

  • Action, event, tracking, transcription, or other label schema
  • Clip, frame, timestamp, or segment-boundary requirements
  • Written annotation guidance and issue-escalation process
  • Metadata fields relevant to the buyer's model and evaluation needs

Quality assurance

  • Video and audio usability checks agreed for the project
  • Temporal-label review and acceptance-criteria process
  • Sampling, feedback, correction, and version-control approach
  • Clear treatment of out-of-scope or unusable material

Consent and provenance

  • Source and collection-route documentation appropriate to the scope
  • Written consent workflow when identifiable contributors are involved
  • Early flagging of biometric, child-participant, sensitive-setting, or re-identification questions
  • Buyer-facing provenance and data-handling notes agreed for delivery

Frequently asked questions

Questions about African video data.

Do you offer a ready-made video-data catalogue?

No. Most video-data work is commissioned around the buyer's actions, scenarios, capture conditions, temporal annotation requirements, and delivery needs. We confirm the practical scope before committing to a project.

Can a project include identifiable people or children?

Potentially, but this is a special project question. The brief should explain why identifiable participants are needed, their role, how the footage will be used, and what safeguards are required. We use written consent workflows where applicable and flag biometric, child-participant, sensitive-setting, and re-identification questions early; buyers should obtain independent professional advice for their own legal and governance obligations.

Can you work with footage we already have?

Yes. If you already hold source footage, we can discuss a preparation, annotation, quality-assurance, and delivery scope around an agreed schema and review process. See the Annotation and Labelling Services page.

Can the collection cover a particular African market or setting?

Tell us the market, setting, participants, activities, capture conditions, and delivery requirement. We assess the brief and confirm what is practical before making a commitment; this page does not claim universal market coverage.

Can the project include speech or transcripts?

Yes, where this is within the agreed scope and the participation, data-use, and quality requirements are clear. If the project needs dedicated voice data, see the African Speech Data page for the related route.
